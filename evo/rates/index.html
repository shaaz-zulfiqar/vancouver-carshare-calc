How the caps work

You pay $0.49 for each minute until the charge reaches the hourly rate. That happens at about 37 minutes, so a trip of 37 minutes or more costs the same as an hour.

The same thing happens at the day rate. Past roughly 5½ hours, one day at $104.99 is cheaper than paying by the hour.

Quick answers: Evo fees and sign-up minutes

Does Evo offer free driving minutes when you sign up?

Yes, with a promo code. The code on this page waives Evo’s one-time $35 registration fee and adds 60 minutes of driving time for eligible new members. Evo’s own refer-a-friend program gives a new member a free membership and $25 in driving minutes instead. Evo says promo credits apply automatically to your next trip, usually expire after 30 days, and can’t be combined with other offers.

What is the Evo membership fee or annual fee?

Evo charges a one-time $35 registration fee when your membership is approved, plus a $2 annual Car Share Operator fee that Evo says it is required to charge. There are no monthly fees. A promo code, or a BCAA membership, waives the $35 registration fee.

What is the $2.24 charge on Evo?

Most likely it is Evo’s $2 annual Car Share Operator fee with 12% GST and PST added ($2.00 + $0.24). Evo doesn’t say how that fee is taxed, so check the line in the app. It isn’t the per-trip All-Access fee: that is $1.85, or $2.07 with 12% tax, and it appears on every trip. Trips longer than 8 hours also add the $1.50 Passenger Vehicle Rental Tax, and ending a trip at YVR adds a $5 drop-off fee.

How much does Evo cost for 1 hour or 1 day?

One hour costs $22.22: the $17.99 hourly rate plus the $1.85 All-Access fee plus 12% tax. One day (24 hours) costs $121.24: the $104.99 daily rate plus the $1.85 fee, $12.82 tax, and $1.58 Passenger Vehicle Rental Tax ($1.50 plus 5% GST). These use Evo’s published rates, effective March 1, 2026.
